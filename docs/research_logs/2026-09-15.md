# September 15: Vehicle maintenance input review

The vehicle defaults now use **$656.33/year for ICE** and **$324.33/year for EV**
repair and maintenance, retaining unrounded values in code. Each allowance
averages 12 years at 12,000 miles/year. Publication reports remain unchanged.
This change follows the accounting correction committed as `c5d6b37`.

## Source and derivation

The cited [ICCT working paper, page 7](https://theicct.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/EV-equity-feb2021.pdf)
uses $0.028/mile for new gasoline vehicles and $0.012/mile for new EVs, with
higher costs as odometer mileage rises. Its source is Harto (2020), Consumer
Reports. The ICCT host rejected retrieval during this check; the same paper was
read from this [public copy](https://lindseyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/NHTSA-2021-0053-1578-Exhibit-86-Bauer-et-al-2021.pdf).

The old defaults can be reconstructed from the cited FHWA 2022 California
totals: [315,244 million annual miles](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/policyinformation/statistics/2022/vm2.cfm)
divided by [31,119,113 registered motor vehicles](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/policyinformation/statistics/2022/mv1.cfm)
gives 10,130.237 miles/vehicle/year. Multiplying by the new-vehicle rates gives
$283.65 and $121.56 after rounding. This reconstruction matches both amounts;
the repository does not retain the original calculation worksheet. The ratio
covers all motor vehicles, not specifically the modeled household vehicles.

The revised allowances use the fuel model's existing 12,000 miles/year and
both appliance classes' existing 12-year service life. They assume a vehicle
starts new and reaches 144,000 miles. Consumer Reports' [Table 2.1](https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Maintenance-Cost-White-Paper-9.24.20-1.pdf)
supplies these repair and maintenance rates:

| Odometer miles | ICE, $/mile | BEV, $/mile | Miles used in this study |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0–50,000 | 0.028 | 0.012 | 50,000 |
| 50,000–100,000 | 0.060 | 0.028 | 50,000 |
| 100,000–200,000 | 0.079 | 0.043 | 44,000 |

This gives $7,876 for ICE and $3,892 for EV over 12 years. Dividing by 12
produces the annual defaults. The code stores these two arithmetic expressions
in the appliance modules. Step 14 retains its existing ledger integration.
Explicit allowance overrides, including zero, remain supported.

## Effect and limitations

Relative to the corrected accounting with the former maintenance defaults,
ICE operating costs increase by $372.68/year and EV costs by $202.77/year.
The ICE-minus-EV maintenance difference increases from $162.09 to $332/year.
Thus the isolated effect favors electrification by $169.91/year. These are
arithmetic effects, not refreshed county classifications. Vehicle costs cancel
within each household's solar/storage adoption comparison.

The constant average ignores repair timing. For a separate timing check, apply
the bands to each year's 12,000 miles. Discount the resulting 12 annual payments
at 7%, then divide by the 12-year annuity factor. Equivalent annual maintenance
is $601.88 for ICE and $291.82 for EV. The flat means exceed these by $54.45 and
$32.52; their difference is $21.93/year. This check covers one new vehicle over
12 years, not a full 25-year replacement or household-cost calculation.

Consumer Reports used 2019 and 2020 survey responses and flags limited evidence
for high-mileage BEVs. We retain source-dollar rates without inflation adjustment.
They are broad benchmarks, not estimates for a particular county or midsize SUV.
The fixed allowance is calibrated to 12,000 miles/year and 12 years. Other
ownership patterns need an explicit revised allowance. EV charging remains an
independent load-profile input; this change does not establish matched driving
distances between that profile and the fuel model.

## Validation and remaining work

New hand-calculated tests cover defaults through appliance, ledger, and reporting
totals, plus custom allowances and service lives. Both default tests failed
before the change; the four override cases already passed. Existing accounting
regression tests remain unchanged.
The baseline suite passed 865 tests with three skips. The final suite passes
871 tests with three skips and the same pre-existing plotting-font warning.

The [isolated validation](../../analysis_results/vehicle_maintenance_c5d6b37/README.md)
checks all 1,692 ledger rows across 47 counties and three incentive cases.
Exactly 141 ICE and 141 EV rows change in maintenance, operating costs, and
lifetime totals. All other fields match the prior accounting-correction ledgers.
The actual Step 14 entry point also passes for both households in Alameda,
Los Angeles, and San Diego. Source, code, and output hashes are recorded.

Alpine's gasoline input remains the next correction. Saved cost reports must
then be regenerated before citing whole-household electrification savings.
The maintenance change does not require new solar/storage optimization.
