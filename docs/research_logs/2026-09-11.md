# Research results and claim assessment — 2026-09-11

The core statewide rerun supports the current-cost storage finding and confirms
Claims 2 and 3 with updated numbers. It does not refresh the ITC sensitivity
in Claim 1 or establish an electrification “package deal” effect.

## Evidence and scope

- Model commit: `8a98b96bc174c93884eed63d013859cf5fa24c6a`.
- Run: `analysis_results/runs/2026-09-11_8a98b96`.
- Coverage: one modeled single-family detached household in each of 47 counties.
  County summaries are unweighted. Each case uses all 8,760 hours.
- Paper scenarios: `baseline_ice_car`, `full_electric_ev`, and
  `full_electric_ev_coopt`. The supporting `baseline` was also regenerated.
- Assumptions: 2026 NBT tariffs and post-ITC capital costs; no ACC Plus;
  annual exports no greater than imports; annual base-credit settlement by
  eligible utility pool. The study lasts 25 years at a 7% discount rate.
  Batteries last 15 years, with replacement and one-third remaining value
  for the replacement battery at year 25.

Equivalent annual cost (EAC) includes annualized equipment costs, household
energy bills, and vehicle operating costs. It includes solar, storage,
appliances, and vehicles. See the [methods](../RESEARCH_METHODS.md).

## Claim 1: Storage economics under NEM 3

**Assessment: the current-cost finding is supported and now covers 47 modeled
counties. The older ITC and battery-price sensitivity results were not rerun.**

The fully electrified optimized case selects zero storage in 46 counties.
San Diego selects a saved capacity of **0.03 kWh (30 Wh)**. No county exceeds
the existing Claim 1 display threshold of 0.1 kWh. This threshold describes
results; it is not a minimum capacity imposed on the optimizer.

The four case-study counties remain within that threshold: Alameda, Fresno,
and Los Angeles select zero; San Diego selects 0.03 kWh. The older claim-page
statement that their maximum capacity is 0.00 kWh needs updating.

Optimized solar ranges from **1.24 to 2.95 kW**, with a **1.79 kW median**.
The model therefore selects small solar systems with negligible storage under
the declared costs. Continuous sizing permits capacities below commercial
unit sizes. Do not interpret the San Diego result as a product recommendation.

Suggested wording:

> At the modeled 2026 post-ITC costs under NEM 3, fully electrified households
> select negligible battery capacity across all 47 modeled counties. The
> largest saved capacity is 0.03 kWh; median solar capacity is 1.79 kW.

The existing Claim 1 also states that the 2025 ITC changes storage selection in
two of four cases and reports battery-price thresholds. Those values retain
their earlier model versions and resolution limits. This run cannot update
them or attribute low storage selection to NEM 3 or ITC withdrawal separately.
The NEM 2 comparison was not rerun either. Refresh matched sensitivities only
if those quantitative policy or price statements remain part of the paper.

## Claim 2: Electrification versus the gas/ICE reference

**Assessment: the conclusion is confirmed for the defined scenario comparison.**

The optimized all-electric household and electric vehicle (EV) cost less than
the gas-appliance and internal-combustion vehicle (ICE) reference in
**46 of 47 counties**. The median reduction is **13.5%**; the mean is **13.1%**.
Del Norte has the largest reduction, **24.3%**. Alpine is the exception:
the optimized all-electric case costs **3.8% more**, about **$579/year**.

Suggested wording:

> The fully electrified household with an EV and optimized solar/storage has
> lower modeled annual cost than the gas-appliance/ICE reference in 46 of 47
> counties, with a median reduction of 13.5%.

The reference includes fixed solar and a 13.5 kWh battery. Its PV size follows
the annual-load matching convention. Claim 2 compares complete household
packages; the change includes appliances, vehicles, solar/storage sizing, and
dispatch. It does not isolate the effect of electrification alone or compare
against a gas household without solar/storage.

## Claim 3: Value of co-optimizing solar and storage

**Assessment: the conclusion is confirmed in all 47 counties.**

Holding the fully electrified appliance mix and EV fixed, optimizing solar,
storage, and dispatch reduces EAC in **47 of 47 counties**. Savings have a
**$2,010/year median** and **$1,987/year mean**. They range from **$1,300/year
in San Diego** to **$2,445/year in Humboldt**.

Suggested wording:

> Jointly optimizing solar/storage capacities and hourly dispatch lowers
> modeled annual cost in all 47 counties relative to the fixed all-electric
> system, with a median saving of $2,010/year.

The fixed case uses annual-load-matched PV and a 13.5 kWh battery. The result
measures the combined change in sizing and dispatch. It does not estimate
sizing alone, dispatch alone, or the benefit of adopting storage. The large
saving is compatible with the optimizer choosing negligible storage.

## Implications for publication

Use the narrower current-cost wording for Claim 1 and the refreshed numbers
for Claims 2 and 3. These conclusions are supported by the completed run.
The generated `claim1.html`, `claim2.html`, and `claim3.html` pages were not
rebuilt by this documentation update. Their older numbers and methods text
need a publication refresh, including the older accounting description in
`figure_builder/README.md`. Retain the provenance of sensitivities not rerun.

The separate package-effect question asks whether electrification increases
solar/storage adoption savings. It requires matched adoption comparisons
before and after electrification, as defined in the methods. Claims 2 and 3
do not supply that comparison. This is a separate claim to establish if used;
it is not a prerequisite for reporting the narrower findings above.

Compared with cached outputs evaluated through the current EAC collector:

| Scenario | Median change in annual cost | County range |
|---|---:|---:|
| Gas appliances + ICE, fixed solar/storage | +$6.27 | $0.00 to +$15.06 |
| All electric + EV, fixed solar/storage | +$27.91 | $0.00 to +$51.11 |
| All electric + EV, optimized solar/storage | +$2.49 | −$0.71 to +$6.76 |

The optimized results remain close to the cache under this accounting basis.
This comparison includes earlier accounting changes. It does not reconstruct
the earlier published HTML results or isolate the effect of commit `8a98b96`.

The annual export cap is nonbinding in all **141 paper scenario/county cases**.
The smallest annual import-minus-export difference is **374.82 kWh**.
This describes the accepted solutions; it does not prove that removing the cap
would leave every optimum unchanged.

All **141 annual electricity bills** replay from saved meter flows within
**$0.001**. The strict statewide validator passed **49 checks**, with no failures.
The full test suite passed **746 tests**, with **3 skipped**, in both tested
SciPy environments. All **847 reused input files** retain their recorded hashes.
The maximum annual capital difference from two-decimal capacity storage is
**$1.37**, in San Mateo. No model or test expectations changed during the run.

Auxiliary payback outputs are excluded from these conclusions. Their optimized
EV case selects the plain baseline instead of the ICE vehicle reference.
Review that selection before citing payback. The pipeline regenerated these
CSVs in the shared `data/results` path; copies are retained with this run.

## Runtime and reproducibility

The recorded run took about **3 hours 11 minutes**. Forty-four county jobs ran
with six workers; their median duration was about 13 minutes. San Diego needed
solver troubleshooting and repeated physical meter-direction constraint rounds.
Its original HiGHS result failed the numerical flow check and was rejected.
The accepted CBC result passed the unchanged physical and billing checks.

Accepted optimizer runtimes were SciPy 1.11.4 / HiGHS 1.2.0 for 44 counties,
SciPy 1.16.1 / HiGHS 1.8.0 for Alameda and Los Angeles, and CBC 2.10.3 for
San Diego. Alameda and Los Angeles hourly outputs agreed across HiGHS versions
to numerical precision. HiGHS used the committed relative gap of `1e-6`;
CBC used its committed defaults. Record these choices when reproducing the
run. Failed and unused trial logs are preserved. No research assumptions changed.
