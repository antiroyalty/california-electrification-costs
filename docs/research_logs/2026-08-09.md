## Net-surplus compensation (NSC): required follow-up

Conceptually, NSC applies when annual exported energy exceeds annual imported
energy:

`net_surplus_kwh = max(annual_export_kwh - annual_import_kwh, 0)`

Under NBT, monthly Energy Export Credits have already been earned on those
exports. At true-up, the utility must identify the energy associated with the
annual surplus, reverse or reconcile the EEC dollars already credited for
that same energy according to its tariff, and then pay the applicable NSC
rate. Remaining dollar credit balances and their carry-forward treatment must
also follow the utility tariff. A single scalar `surplus_kwh × nsc_rate`
inserted at the end of the existing bill would double-count compensation and
is therefore not an acceptable implementation.

The intended domain design is:

- `tariffs/true_up.py`
  - `TrueUpPolicy`: utility-specific settlement rules;
  - `NetSurplusCompensationSchedule`: effective/monthly NSC prices and sources;
  - `TrueUpSettlement`: surplus kWh, EEC reconciliation, NSC credit, remaining
    credit banks, and audit fields.
- `data/tariffs/nsc_rates.csv`: utility, effective or true-up month, rate in
  USD/kWh, and source ID.
- `TariffBundle.true_up_policy`: the resolved policy supplied to billing.

Before implementing this, source-lock the exact PG&E, SCE, and SDG&E NSC
price-selection and EEC-reconciliation rules. The current code is asymmetric:
it raises only for SDG&E annual net exporters and separately zeros SCE/SDG&E
base credit banks. That behavior is a known incomplete placeholder, not a
research assumption to preserve.
