contains 94 fresh optimizations and 188 cost cells, using all 8,760 hours.
It compares `baseline_ice_car_coopt` and `full_electric_ev_coopt`.

## Implications for the three claims

1. **Storage economics:** both households select zero battery in 46 counties.
   San Diego selects 0.02 kWh for gas/ICE and 0.03 kWh for electric/EV.
   Median solar sizes are 1.22 and 1.79 kW, respectively.
   The evidence supports a solar finding with negligible storage at these costs.
   It does not isolate the effect of NEM 3 from ITC withdrawal.
2. **Electrification economics — on hold:** in the original output, electric/EV costs
   less in **36 counties**, more in **10**, and is numerically unresolved in **one**.
   Median savings are **$330.90/year**. San Benito's $0.86 saving is within its
   $2.36 numerical bound. Without equipment, median electrification savings are
   only $0.45/year, with 24 positive and 23 negative differences.
   Letting each household decline unprofitable adoption preserves the 36/10/1
   classification and $330.90 median. The older 46/47, 13.5% headline used fixed
   gas-household equipment; it cannot describe this matched comparison. The vehicle
   discrepancy described below invalidates both as publication-ready cost headlines.

   a separate result. This run does not refresh the fixed-system case or its

## Vehicle-cost discrepancy found during review

**Implementation follow-up:** Step 14 now copies the vehicle's itemized operating
costs, and the redundant ICE estimator has been removed. Regression tests cover
default, custom, and zero maintenance costs and propagate fuel errors. The saved
run below is unchanged; Alpine's input and refreshed cost reports remain pending.

`ICEVehicleAppliance` declares annual maintenance of $283.65. Its
former `get_annual_operating_cost_estimate` added a separate $1,200 default.
The old Step 14 subtracted $283.65 to label the remainder as fuel, then added
$283.65 maintenance and insurance. The saved total therefore uses $1,200 maintenance.
It exceeds the same object's `get_cost_breakdown` by **$916.35/year** in all
47 counties. The reporting collector passes this amount into household costs.

Subtracting that discrepancy from both gas-household cells is an arithmetic
diagnostic, not an implemented correction. It changes the optimized comparison
from 36/10/1 to **5 cheaper and 42 more expensive**, with median electrification
savings of **−$585.45/year**. Allowing adoption to be declined gives the same
counts and median. All adoption differences and the package effect stay unchanged.

Alpine's gasoline price is explicitly zero despite positive vehicle mileage.
Do not interpret its fuel or whole-household cost result as realistic.
The remaining follow-up must require valid fuel inputs and regenerate affected
cost reports. Changing only these vehicle-dollar inputs does not require new
solar/storage optimizations.
The older fixed-gas comparison has the same upstream exposure.

Energy spending is a separate comparison from total household cost. Using the
vehicle's itemized gasoline calculation, electricity plus natural gas plus
gasoline costs less after electrification in all 46 counties with positive
gasoline-price inputs. Median savings are $1,673/year without solar and
$2,137/year with optimized solar. These exclude capital, maintenance, and
insurance; Alpine is excluded because its gasoline price is zero. EV charging
is already in electricity bills. This does not isolate building electrification
from the vehicle change.

## Validation and bounded follow-ups

All **98 statewide checks** pass. All **94 bills** replay within $0.001
(maximum error below $0.000000000002). Every accepted solver gap is below $1/year.
The largest paired numerical bound, including saved-capacity rounding, is
$3.62/year. These bounds do not cover modeling uncertainty.
The full suite changed from **853 passed, 3 skipped** before edits to
**856 passed, 3 skipped**. Existing expectations remain unchanged.
These checks validated bill and comparison arithmetic, but did not detect the
upstream vehicle-cost discrepancy. Passing them does not validate that assumption.

Retail billing retains the source calendar; NEM 3 uses 2026. Rebilling retail
on the aligned 2026 calendar changes individual bills by at most $8.13/year
and the package effect by at most $3.64/year. All 47 signs remain positive;
the median becomes $309.11. Calendar unification is a bounded reporting follow-up.

The run reached validated completion in about 53 minutes, excluding the later
attribution correction. Accepted San Diego solves took 39 seconds for gas/ICE
and 99 seconds for electric/EV. Gas/ICE required an explicit use of the existing
bulk meter-constraint path after rejected retries. Default triggering remains
unchanged. Exposing or adjusting that trigger is an engineering follow-up.

Failed trials are retained. Initially missing price-audit files were generated
from unchanged inputs before validation. An initial attribution diagnostic used
unaligned loads; its outputs are archived as invalid. The corrected diagnostic
verifies alignment against all 94 source profiles. Primary costs did not change.
