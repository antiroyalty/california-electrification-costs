Controlled comparison

Current post-ITC capital-cost inputs are $3,300/kW for PV and $1,460.64/kWh for storage. The 2025 ITC sensitivity uses $2,310/kW and $1,022.448/kWh.

The NEM 2 model applies annual retail-dollar credit netting, interval non-bypassable charges, positive monthly net-consumption recovery charges, credit expiration, and net-surplus compensation. The NBT optimization uses the source-locked hourly Energy Export Credit, ACC Plus, and the complete retail import schedule.

The targeted Alameda 8,760-hour NEM 2 check selects 7.317 kW PV and 0.000 kWh storage. Its PV result differs from the common-resolution observation by 0.0000 kW.

Interpretation boundaries

  1. This is a four-county case study.

    Alameda, Fresno, Los Angeles, and San Diego represent three investor-owned utilities. The result is not a statewide adoption estimate.

  2. The comparison is not a historical NEM 2 bill reconstruction.

    Both export regimes use the same source-locked 2026 retail import rates. This design isolates compensation rules from historical rate changes.

  3. The common comparison uses a weighted 12×24 chronology.

    Only the Alameda NEM 2 current-cost cell has a dedicated 8,760-hour validation. The other three NEM 2 cells still require exact checks before publication.

  4. NEM 2 PV reaches the eligibility ceiling.

    The result establishes the optimum within the modeled tariff domain. It does not identify the unconstrained NEM 2 PV optimum above 100% annual-load coverage.

  5. The NBT sizing objective does not reproduce every monthly settlement rule.

    It values hourly imports and exports with the source-locked schedules. The separate annual-bill path applies the complete generation/delivery, non-bypassable charge, credit-bank, and true-up primitives.
