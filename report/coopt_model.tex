\section{Overview}

Step~9b is a linear program that jointly chooses the rooftop solar array size, the
battery energy and power capacity, and the hour-by-hour dispatch of solar, battery, and
grid, so as to minimize a household's total annual cost. Total annual cost is the sum of
annualized capital cost and the net annual energy bill (imports priced at the retail
time-of-use rate, exports credited at the NEM~3.0 Avoided Cost Calculator rate). The
program is solved at full 8760-hour resolution, or on a reduced set of 288 representative
time slices (12~months $\times$ 24~hours) weighted by days per month. All variables are
continuous; there are no integer variables.

\section{Optimization problem}

\paragraph{Objective.} Minimize total annual cost: annualized capital cost, plus the net
annual energy bill (imports minus export credits), plus battery degradation cost.
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min\ \ & PV\,c_{pv}\,\alpha_{pv} \;+\; B_E\,c_{b}\,\alpha_{b} \;+\; B_P\,c_{bp}\,\alpha_{b} \\[2pt]
& {}+ \sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}} w_h\Big[\big(x_h^{g\to l}+x_h^{g\to b}\big)\,p_h^{imp}
        \;-\; \big(x_h^{pv\to g}+x_h^{b\to g}\big)\,p_h^{exp}\Big] \\[2pt]
& {}+ \sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}} w_h\,c_{deg}\big(x_h^{b\to l}+x_h^{b\to g}\big)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Constraints.} For every hour $h\in\mathcal{H}$:
\begin{align}
x_h^{pv\to l}+x_h^{pv\to b}+x_h^{pv\to g} &\;\le\; PV\cdot G_h && \text{(solar availability)} \\
x_h^{pv\to l}+x_h^{b\to l}+x_h^{g\to l} &\;=\; L_h && \text{(load balance)} \\
x_h^{pv\to b}+x_h^{g\to b} &\;\le\; B_P && \text{(charge power)} \\
x_h^{b\to l}+x_h^{b\to g} &\;\le\; B_P && \text{(discharge power)} \\
soc_{h+1} &\;=\; soc_h + \eta_{ch}\big(x_h^{pv\to b}+x_h^{g\to b}\big)
        - \tfrac{1}{\eta_{dis}}\big(x_h^{b\to l}+x_h^{b\to g}\big) && \text{(SOC dynamics)} \\
\underline{s}\,B_E \;\le\; soc_h &\;\le\; \overline{s}\,B_E && \text{(SOC bounds)}
\end{align}
The state of charge is cyclic: $soc$ returns to its starting value over the full year in
8760 mode, or over each representative day in 288 mode. By default grid charging is
disabled ($x_h^{g\to b}=0$) and battery-to-grid export is permitted.

\section{Coefficients and conventions}
\label{sec:coef}

The capital-annualization factors convert an up-front capital cost into an equivalent
annual cost over the analysis horizon $N$:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{pv} = \mathrm{CRF}(r,N),
\qquad
\alpha_{b} = \frac{K_{batt}}{\mathrm{PVA}(r,N)},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CRF}(r,n) = \frac{r(1+r)^{n}}{(1+r)^{n}-1},
\qquad
\mathrm{PVA}(r,N) = \frac{1-(1+r)^{-N}}{r},
\qquad
K_{batt} = 1 + (1+r)^{-n_{batt}}.
\end{equation}
Solar is amortized once over its $N$-year life. The battery term $K_{batt}$ is the present
value of \emph{two} purchases: one at installation and a replacement at year $n_{batt}$
within the $N$-year horizon. Because of that replacement, $\alpha_{b} > \mathrm{CRF}(r,n_{batt})$.

\paragraph{Prices and rates.} $p_h^{exp}$ is the NEM~3.0 Avoided Cost Calculator export
credit, supplied as a month~$\times$~hour table per utility. $p_h^{imp}$ is the retail
time-of-use import price for the county's utility and rate plan.

\paragraph{Defaults.} $r = 7\%$, $N = 25$~yr, $n_{batt} = 15$~yr, round-trip efficiency
$\eta_{ch}\eta_{dis} = 0.96$, state of charge confined to 20\%--90\% of $B_E$, and
$c_{bp} = c_{deg} = 0$. The 15-year battery life and battery cost follow NREL ATB~2024
Residential Battery Storage; solar cost follows the CPUC-adopted installed price.

\paragraph{Scope of the objective bill.} The bill inside the objective is a simplified
hourly accounting (imports at $p_h^{imp}$, exports at $p_h^{exp}$) used to drive the
sizing and dispatch decision. The full NEM~3.0 monthly accounting (non-bypassable
charges, fixed charges, minimum bill, carry-forward credits, and annual true-up) is
applied downstream (Step~12) when computing the reported bill.

\section{Incentive policy regime}
\label{sec:incentives}

The capital costs $c_{pv}$ and $c_{b}$ entering the objective are \emph{net} of whichever
incentives legally exist in the modeled installation year. Which incentives exist is a
policy question separate from how much of an available incentive a given household
actually captures, and the model treats the two as orthogonal axes: a \emph{policy
regime} determines the set of live incentives, and an \emph{incentive scenario}
(full / half / none) scales what is captured.

Two regimes are defined. Under \textsc{itc\_2025}, the federal residential clean energy
credit (IRC~25D) supplies 30\% of installed cost, giving $c_{pv} = \$2{,}310$/kW and
$c_{b} = \$1{,}022$/kWh. Under \textsc{post\_itc\_2026}, IRC~25D is repealed and the
gross costs apply directly: $c_{pv} = \$3{,}300$/kW and $c_{b} = \$1{,}461$/kWh. The
One Big Beautiful Bill Act (Pub.~L.~119-21, 2025-07-04) repealed IRC~25D for
expenditures made after 2025-12-31, so \textsc{post\_itc\_2026} is current law and is
the default regime for this analysis. The ITC era is retained as an explicit comparison
regime rather than as the headline case.

\paragraph{Direction of effect.} The repeals do not all push the same way. Removing
IRC~25D raises the cost of solar and storage, which makes storage \emph{less} likely to
enter the optimal solution and therefore reinforces the finding that storage does not
pencil out under NEM~3.0. Removing IRC~25C and IRC~30D raises the cost of the heat pump,
the heat pump water heater, and the vehicle, which makes full electrification more
expensive relative to a gas baseline and therefore cuts \emph{against} the finding that
electrification is cost-competitive. The two effects are separated in the results by
applying the repeals in sequence rather than jointly, so that each change in outcome is
attributable to a specific policy change.

\paragraph{Regime boundary.} The two-regime formulation is an approximation at the
boundary. IRC~30D terminated on 2025-09-30, roughly three months before IRC~25C and
IRC~25D, so no single date cleanly separates the regimes. For a 2026-vintage analysis,
in which all three credits have lapsed, the approximation has no effect on results.
